# 💎 Crystal Capital Index & Internal Tokenomics
### TON Smart-Contract Infrastructure | Capital Allocation & NAV Accounting Vehicle

This document details the architecture of the Crystal Capital Index, designed as an internal tokenized investment vehicle and smart-contract accounting mechanism deployed on the TON network.

---

## 🏛 1. Core Concept & Structural Design

* **Internal Allocation Vehicle:** The Crystal Capital Index does not operate as a publicly traded speculative market asset. It functions strictly as an institutional internal investment unit representing a proportional share of the fund's managed capital pools.
* **Net Asset Value (NAV) Pricing:** The value of the internal index token is mathematically tied to the aggregate performance and real-time equity of the fund's systematic quant execution and asset-backed allocations.
* **Smart-Contract Foundation:** Deployed on the TON network to ensure low-latency settlements, transparent on-chain accounting, and automated high-water mark verification.

---

## ⚙️ 2. Investment Flow & Settlement Architecture

[ Capital Inflow (USDT/TON) ] ➔ [ Smart-Contract Minting ] ➔ [ Direct Quant Allocation ]
                                                                       │
[ Profit Generation / NAV Increase ] ◄─────────────────────────────────┘
                │
                ▼
[ Instant Liquidity Redemption ] ➔ [ Smart-Contract Burning ] ➔ [ Capital Return ]

1. **Capital Entry:** Qualified participants allocate capital into the internal smart contract, receiving proportional index units priced at current NAV.
2. **Systematic Deployment:** Underlying capital is routed into non-custodial quant execution strategies and verified RWA operational pipelines.
3. **Yield Accrual:** Positive alpha generated by quant models increases the total equity of the pool, automatically elevating the token NAV.
4. **Redemption & Liquidity:** Index units can be burned via the internal smart contract for principal and profit withdrawal without lock-up penalties for liquid pools.
